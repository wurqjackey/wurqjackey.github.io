Novel Framework and Benchmarks for Multi-View Pedestrian Association and Tracking on Mobile Aerial-Ground Platforms
Ruiqi Wu, Bingliang Jiao, Ruize Han, Hangzheng Yu, Xunkai Jiang, Shining Wang, Yuanqi Hu, Wenxuan Wang, Peng Wang
Manuscript Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, 2026

We present FUSION, a viewpoint-robust framework for multi-view pedestrian association and tracking under mobile aerial-ground settings, together with RealMvMoAT, a large-scale benchmark featuring substantial viewpoint shifts, diverse scene layouts, and complex platform motion.

Overview

Multi-view Multi-object Association and Tracking (MvMoAT) aims to associate the same pedestrian across camera views while maintaining temporally consistent trajectories. This problem becomes particularly challenging in mobile aerial-ground systems, where dynamic platforms and large viewpoint shifts often cause severe appearance distortion.

To address this issue, we propose FUSION framework, with MAC, a multi-cue adaptive combination module that combines appearance information with local context, 3D body structure, and neighbor relations for robust cross-view identity association. We further introduce RMFS, a real-time multi-view feature synchronization module that aggregates historical and cross-view features to improve temporal tracking stability. In addition, we build RealMvMoAT, a large-scale real-world benchmark collected from mobile aerial and ground platforms. It provides a challenging testbed for cross-view association and tracking under realistic viewpoint variation.

Method: FUSION

FUSION consists of a backbone and two main modules: MAC for multi-cue adaptive fusion in cross-view association, and RMFS for cross-view and temporal feature synchronization in tracking.

Our Proposed FUSION Framework

MAC: Multi-cue Adaptive Combination

MAC extracts and integrates multiple complementary cues at the similarity level. It is designed to address the instability caused by challenges like large viewpoint changes.

RMFS: Real-time Multi-view Feature Synchronization

RMFS maintains identity-level historical representations and dynamically refines trajectory features, enabling more stable temporal matching between new detections and existing trajectories.

Why it works

Instead of relying only on appearance, FUSION leverages scene context, body geometry, and spatial relations to improve identity reasoning across views and over time.

Multi-cue Design

Appearance

Provides the primary visual identity representation and serves as the basic cue for matching.

Local Context

Captures semantic information from the surrounding region, helping disambiguate identities when appearance is unreliable.

3D Structure

Uses body pose and shape as intrinsic cues that remain relatively stable across viewpoints.

Neighbor Relation

Models the local spatial arrangement among nearby pedestrians to provide robust relational evidence.

RealMvMoAT Benchmark

RealMvMoAT is a large-scale benchmark designed for multi-view pedestrian association and tracking under realistic mobile aerial-ground settings. It contains diverse outdoor scenes, multiple synchronized views, rich viewpoint variation, and irregular camera motion.

Key Characteristics

  • Captured by up to 7 synchronized cameras, including 5 UAV views and 2 ground views
  • Diverse outdoor environments such as parks, lawns, lakesides, sports fields, and urban plazas
  • Irregular platform motion including circular sweeps, hovering rotations, lateral panning, and zigzag flight
  • Supports both cross-view association and temporal tracking under realistic deployment conditions

Annotation Pipeline

  • Temporal alignment through clipping and offset correction
  • Hybrid box annotation with manual labeling and detector-assisted initialization
  • Fully manual cross-view identity assignment
  • Multi-stage quality control for consistency, blur, occlusion, and crowded scenes

Experimental Results

FUSION achieves state-of-the-art performance on RealMvMoAT and also demonstrates strong generalization on multiple public MvMoAT benchmarks. The gains come from jointly improving cross-view association and temporal identity consistency.

Main Findings

  • FUSION substantially outperforms strong baselines on the proposed RealMvMoAT benchmark
  • Adaptive multi-cue fusion improves robustness under appearance ambiguity and viewpoint change
  • RMFS strengthens trajectory consistency by synchronizing features across views and time
  • The method generalizes well to public datasets with different camera layouts and motion settings
